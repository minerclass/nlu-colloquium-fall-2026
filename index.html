Pedagogical Frictionin the Age of Generative AI and Tertiary Algorithmicity

A qualitative-dominant convergent mixed methods study

Micah J. Miner Doctoral Candidate, Ed.D. in Curriculum, Advocacy, and Policy National Louis University · Chair: Dr. Terry Jo Smith

Approved as exempt by the NLU IRB, protocol ER01884. This is research in progress. No findings are reported.

Research topic · The problem

Generative AI can produce the outputs of learning without the learning

What can now be generated

Fluent text, code, images, and analyses

Outputs that may be difficult to distinguish from human-authored work

What learning involves

Effort, dialogue, authorship, judgment, and participation

The process the product no longer shows

“The problem is that schools lack a sufficiently precise empirical and conceptual language for judging which forms of ease support access by removing exclusionary friction and which forms of ease bypass learning by removing productive friction.”

Dissertation proposal, Chapter One (defended August 13, 2026)

Purpose

Four vantage points on the same change

Click a group

Generative AI and the conditions of learning
“The purpose of this qualitative-dominant convergent mixed methods study is to investigate how classroom-facing educators, building-level administrators, and district or system-level leaders understand and navigate the effects of generative AI on teaching, learning, authorship, assessment, and institutional decision making, while adult university students contribute retrospective learner perspectives on cognitive engagement, authorship, and disclosure norms.”

Classroom-facing educators describe how AI changes the work students do and how they judge it. They also complete a short scenario card sort.

Building-level administrators describe the school-level guidance, professional learning, and assessment expectations that shape classroom choices.

District or system-level leaders describe the policy, governance, and institutional decisions that enable or constrain classroom practice.

Adult university students contribute “retrospective learner perspectives on cognitive engagement, authorship, and disclosure norms.” They also complete the card sort.

Research questions

Three questions move from experience to conditions to supports

Click a question to see its evidence

RQ1 draws on

Sources. Interviews across all four groups; the scenario card sort for classroom-facing educators and adult university students; educator survey, institutional documents, and selected national evidence as context.

Claim boundary. Situated interpretations of how participants understand these changes. No direct measurement of student learning outcomes.

RQ2 draws on

Sources. Educator and leader interviews; card-sort explanations where relevant; document analysis; the educator survey; selected NCES and RAND evidence.

Claim boundary. Conditions that enable or constrain friction-preserving pedagogy, not causal effects or representative national estimates.

RQ3 draws on

Sources. Integrated RQ1 and RQ2 evidence. No additional participant group or instrument.

Analysis. Cross-role synthesis, joint displays, narrative weaving, and meta-inferences that keep convergence, divergence, expansion, and silence visible.

Theoretical framework · Pedagogical friction

Some friction is worth keeping, and some is worth removing

“the intentional preservation of educationally productive resistance necessary for durable learning under conditions where frictionless automation has become increasingly available”

Productive friction

Difficulty that builds capacity

Supports the intended learning, so it is worth preserving.

Exclusionary friction

Difficulty that blocks access

Creates barriers unrelated to the intended learning, so it is worth removing.

The equity boundary. The study asks whose learning a given difficulty supports and whose participation it obstructs (Annamma et al., 2013; Dolmage, 2017). The framework is not a call to ban AI tools or make school harder. Choose an example.

The same tool use can reduce both kinds of friction at once.

May remove

An exclusionary barrier: writing in an additional language

May complicate

Language choice, voice, reasoning, and authorship

AI may work as accessibility support rather than as a cognitive shortcut.

Depends on

Instructional design, context, and human oversight

The risk

Recoding legitimate accommodation as bypass

Theoretical framework · Structure

Three learner-facing forms rest on one institutional condition

Click a form

Each learner-facing form answers a pressure that AI creates. Choose a form to see the pressure and the practices that preserve it.

Pressure: noetic displacement

When AI substitutes for cognitive work that supports understanding

Preserved through

Generation before solution, retrieval practice, annotated reading, slow writing, argument mapping, worked-example fading, and structured reflection before any AI use

Pressure: rhetorical saturation

When generated language weakens genuine exchange or obscures the source of a claim

Preserved through

Socratic seminars, peer review, oral explanation, conferences, debate, critique protocols, and authentic audiences

Pressure: existential abstraction

When a polished artifact becomes separated from a learner’s willingness or ability to own and defend it

Preserved through

Oral defenses, reflective authorship statements, process memos, place-based inquiry, and assignments that connect claims to a student’s own reasoning

Why it is the condition

Individual teachers cannot preserve the other three alone if grades, pacing guides, device environments, parent expectations, and district policy reward frictionless completion

Includes

AI policy, assessment expectations, professional development priorities, privacy governance, approved-tool ecosystems, leadership language, and schedules that permit slowing learning when it is educationally necessary

Methodology · Grounded in constructivist qualitative inquiry

Qualitative-dominant convergent mixed methods (QUAL + quan)

Click a part of the design

The strands are collected in parallel, analyzed separately, and then brought together. No source validates or overrides another.
Dominant strand. Interpretive codebook thematic analysis: participant-near first-cycle coding, second-cycle themes, temporal coding of adult-student accounts, and attention to discrepant accounts.
Supplemental strand. Descriptive and conditional survey comparisons, run in R and checked in jamovi. No causal claims and no representative national estimates.
Context. Document analysis and national structural context extend or contextualize participant accounts.
Integration. Joint displays and narrative weaving, reading for convergence, divergence, expansion, or silence. Silence counts as a result.
Interpretation. Meta-inferences translate experience and institutional conditions into situated implications. Evidence that complicates the framework is kept in view.

Creswell & Plano Clark (2018); Braun & Clarke (2006, 2021); Saldaña (2021). Analysis details: defense methods matrix
